Research note 11 Sept 2026 Pre-decision

Mitti Labs and Varaha

Two Indian developers, two working playbooks, and one uncomfortable finding for us: neither of them chose to lead with reforestation. Understanding why is the whole point of reading this before we design anything.

The split

Same buyer, opposite architecture

Both are India-based, both sold volume to Google, both raised real money in the last eighteen months. Structurally they are almost inverses of each other — and that contrast is the most useful thing on this page.

Teardown — Mitti Labs

Sell the verification, not the methane

Flooded paddies go anaerobic, anaerobic soil breeds methanogens, and rice becomes one of the largest human methane sources on earth. Alternate Wetting and Drying breaks the cycle by letting the field dry down before re‑flooding. Reported effect: roughly 50% less methane and 40% less irrigation water, with no yield penalty. Methane is ~28× CO2 over a century, so a change in water scheduling throws off real credit volume — no planting, no new asset, no twenty‑year commitment.

The hard part was never the agronomy. It is proving the practice happened on land you never visited. Two claims carry the commercial weight:

Tier 3 emission factors. IPCC accounting has tiers: Tier 1 is a global default from a table, Tier 2 is regional, Tier 3 is model- or measurement-based and specific to local conditions. Mitti builds theirs from direct methane flux measurement and field trials in their own districts, and Gold Standard issued against it.

Census, not sample. Conventional smallholder verification reviews logbooks and interviews for a sample — they cite under 1% of farmers actually reviewed. Mitti monitors every acre remotely, detecting flood and dry cycles, seeding and harvest from orbit.

Buyers are not paying for methane avoidance. They are paying for methane avoidance that will not embarrass them in three years.

What we could copy: the dMRV‑first posture, the census argument, the single‑pathway focus, and the co‑benefit framing — note they raised the Series A on water resilience, not carbon, which widens the funder pool considerably. What we could not copy cheaply: Tier 3 factors are a multi‑year, capital‑intensive science programme. That is precisely why a company founded in 2023 could sign a landmark deal in 2026 — the intervening years went into measurement infrastructure.

Teardown — Varaha

A logistics company wearing a climate-tech logo

Varaha took the inverse bet: build one dMRV and farmer‑operations platform, then run whichever pathway the science and the market currently reward. They now describe themselves as the largest carbon project developer in Asia.

The portfolio is unusually broad. Regenerative agriculture under Verra VM0042 is the flagship Kheti programme — direct‑seeded rice, residue management, reduced tillage. Biochar in Banni, Gujarat converts Prosopis juliflora, an invasive species, into a durable removal — the removal and the biodiversity restoration are the same act. Enhanced rock weathering spread 100,000 t of basalt across smallholder cotton farms and produced Asia's first registry‑backed ERW issuance, third in the world. They are the first company anywhere with verified issuances from two distinct removal pathways.

The stack: Vann, a field‑operations app with a dedicated ARR variant, handling enrolment, plot boundaries and practice evidence; a proprietary Carbon Quantification Tool for GHG accounting; and remote sensing with ML quality control on top. They describe it as zero‑trust — near‑daily satellite and AI checks replacing the annual manual audit.

Choosing app‑plus‑satellite over satellite‑only is a pathway decision, not a taste one. For soil carbon and agroforestry you cannot see the practice from orbit reliably enough. You need a person with a phone at the plot — and then you use the satellite to catch the person.

One financing detail deserves more attention than the headline number: Mirova took no equity. It put in $30.5M and receives a share of credits generated over time. That is a prepay stream, the instrument mining has used for decades. It funds working capital without dilution and moves delivery risk onto the developer.

Transferable

Seven patterns worth stealing

  1. Sell the verification

    Credits are a commodity; trust is not. Both companies' pitch is fundamentally about MRV credibility, and that is what closed the Google deals.

  2. Pick a short evidence loop

    Rice credits within a season, biochar on production, ERW on application. Trees take five to ten years. This is why neither led with reforestation.

  3. The co-benefit drives adoption

    Farmers adopt AWD for water security. Carbon is the financing mechanism behind the curtain. A carbon-only story has a retention problem.

  4. Anchor offtake before scale

    Google-shaped demand turns a science project into a financeable asset. Start that conversation before the hectares, not after them.

  5. Prepay beats equity

    The Mirova structure is available to us: capital advanced against future credits, no dilution.

  6. Registry per pathway

    Gold Standard for rice methane, Verra for soil, Puro for biochar and ERW. Do not marry a registry.

  7. Own the ground truth

    Both built their own field app. No land-based dMRV survives audit on satellites alone.

The arithmetic that kills most projects

Three fixes exist and every serious developer uses all three: aggregate hard — viable clusters start near 50 farmers and 200–500 ha, real projects run to tens of thousands, and aggregators take 30–60% of credit revenue to cover fixed cost; collapse MRV cost with remote sensing, which is exactly why Mitti and Varaha spend their capital there; and choose a higher‑value credit.

Our stated goal

Reforestation is the hardest square on the board

If we build reforestation, the governing document is Verra VM0047 (Afforestation, Reforestation and Revegetation, v1.1, June 2025), now ICVCM CCP‑approved — which is what separates the €28 credit from the €9 one. It is the first nature‑based methodology to use remote sensing for dynamic performance benchmarks: instead of arguing a counterfactual in a project document, the baseline is re‑derived from observed data at every verification. The central measured quantity is the stocking index, and Verra has vetted external data service providers to supply it.

That is genuinely good news for a software‑led team. The rest is not:

Reforestation is the slowest, most capital-hungry, lowest-priced and highest-reversal-risk pathway available.

That is not an argument against doing it. It is an argument against doing it first, alone and unfunded. The pattern that works — Varaha's pattern — is to run a fast‑cycle pathway that generates cashflow and MRV credibility, and let it carry the trees through their pre‑credit years. Agroforestry is the natural bridge: it puts trees on farmland already enrolled for soil carbon, so the enrolment, boundary data, farmer relationship and field app are already paid for.

Known failure modes

Documented, not hypothetical

  • MRV collapse. Northern Rangelands Trust credits in Kenya were suspended over flawed soil‑carbon accounting. The market has learned that soil measurement at scale is genuinely hard.
  • Attrition between listing and issuance. Of agriculture projects listed with Verra in India, only a fraction registered and — per the source — none had issued. Listing is not registration; registration is not issuance. Model on issuance.
  • Farmer follow‑through. Projects fail on weak extension services, especially with marginalised communities. Over 85% of Indian farmers are smallholders with no prior exposure to carbon crediting.
  • Permanence. Unresolved for smallholder land, and buffer pool contributions cut sellable volume from day one.
  • Double counting. With India's CCTS live, the interaction between voluntary credits and the national mechanism is a live legal question.

Policy we must design around. India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme launches mid‑2026, replacing PAT with GHG‑intensity trading. Its offset mechanism for non‑obligated sectors — agriculture and forestry — is being operationalised, the Indian Carbon Market Portal went live in March 2026, and eight BEE‑approved offset methodologies exist so far, including mangrove afforestation. Critically, projects must have a start date no earlier than 1 January 2025. That is a hard gate, and it happens to be in our favour if we start now.

Blocking

Six questions before a line of code

These are genuinely open, and the answers change what gets built. The first, second and fourth are disqualifying — nothing downstream is worth starting without them.

Developer or vendor?

Do we own the credits and the farmer relationships, or sell the MRV stack to people who do? Mitti and Varaha are both developers who built software. The reverse is a different company with a much smaller market today.

What funds years one to four?

Reforestation alone does not. Which fast‑cycle pathway carries the trees?

Voluntary, CCTS, or both?

Different methodologies, different buyers, different price points — and a real risk of conflict between them.

What land, and what tenure evidence?

ARR is unfinanceable without provable long‑horizon rights. Cheapest way to disqualify a plan, so check it first.

Build ground truth, or buy it?

Do we need our own Vann equivalent, or can we license field capture and spend our effort elsewhere?

What is our Tier 3?

What defensible measurement asset do we own? Without one we are reselling commodity satellite indices.

Sequenced by dependency

Proposed next steps

  1. 0Decide

    This week. Answer the six questions above. Everything below is blocked on the first, second and fourth.

  2. 12–4 wks

    Feasibility, desk only. Read VM0047 v1.1 in full plus the CCTS offset methodology list, and write a one‑page eligibility memo for one named candidate landscape.

    Then build a bottom‑up model for that landscape: hectares, species, growth curves, credit timing, buffer deduction, MRV cost per hectare, farmer share. Kill the plan here if it does not clear. Start the offtake conversation in this phase, not in phase 3.

  3. 24–8 wks

    Prototype the MRV core. The only genuinely software part, and the reusable one: a stocking‑index and biomass pipeline over a real area of interest. The literature has converged on a known‑good architecture — Sentinel‑2 optical and Sentinel‑1 SAR fused with GEDI spaceborne LiDAR as sparse height and biomass ground truth, an ML model on top, with explicit uncertainty quantification. Open global canopy height models exist to benchmark against.

    Deliverable: canopy height, biomass and uncertainty for one candidate site, validated against whatever field plots we can obtain.

  4. 3Pilot

    One cluster, real farmers, real enrolment, ground‑truth capture, one verification cycle. Small enough to fail cheaply.
